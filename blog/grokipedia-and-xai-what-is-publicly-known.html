Grokipedia And xAI What Is Publicly Known

xAI has said little about Grokipedia's inner workings. This guide separates confirmed public facts — launch, scale, the 2026 freeze — from the many things nobody outside the company can verify.

By GrokExpedia Editorial Team • Updated 2026-10-07 • 7 min read

Why "publicly known" needs saying

Grokipedia sits at the intersection of two things that generate more speculation than documentation: Elon Musk's public statements and the inner workings of a private AI company. xAI has published little formal documentation about the encyclopedia, Musk's last public post about it was reportedly in February 2026, and the company did not comment on press investigations into its operations. What follows is therefore a careful separation: confirmed public facts, widely reported claims, and the things nobody outside xAI can verify. The distinction is the point — it is also the skill this whole publication exists to teach.

What xAI is

xAI is the artificial intelligence company founded by Elon Musk, announced in July 2023 with the stated mission of understanding the universe. Its flagship product is Grok, a large language model and chatbot integrated with X (formerly Twitter), positioned as a less filtered alternative to competitors. The company has built massive computing infrastructure for training its models and, through Musk's overlapping companies, sits close to X's real-time data stream — an advantage Musk has repeatedly cited for Grok's awareness of current events.

The announcement: September 2025

In September 2025, Musk posted on X that xAI was building an AI-generated encyclopedia — Grokipedia — intended as, in his framing, a massive improvement over Wikipedia. The announcement followed years of public criticism in which Musk called Wikipedia politically biased, at one point discouraging donations to it. The stated rationale was straightforward: an encyclopedia written by AI could be more neutral, more comprehensive, and free of what Musk described as ideological distortion in volunteer-edited reference works. Critics noted the obvious tension — replacing human editorial judgment with a model trained and directed by a single company does not automatically remove bias; it relocates it.

The launch: October 27, 2025

According to public reports and the site's own pages, Grokipedia launched on October 27, 2025 at grokipedia.com as "version 0.1" with 885,279 articles. The rollout stumbled immediately — the site crashed under launch-day traffic before stabilizing later that day — and early coverage from outlets including The Verge noted the absence of public edit histories. Traffic reportedly peaked above 460,000 US daily visits on launch day, then settled to roughly 35,000 daily visits by November 2025.

From the start, reviewers observed that the corpus was mixed: some entries freshly generated by Grok, others adapted from Wikipedia — in some cases closely. Articles could not be edited directly; logged-in visitors could suggest corrections or new articles through a form, with Grok reviewing each submission and also making autonomous changes to articles on its own.

Growth, version 0.2, and the suggestion pipeline

Publicly reported figures trace rapid expansion: roughly 885,000 articles at launch, about 1.7 million by late December 2025, over 1.8 million in early 2026, then a sharp jump past five million by January 9, 2026 — aided by a "Suggest Article" feature introduced in late December 2025 — reaching a displayed count of 6,092,140 by January 10, 2026. That counter has reportedly remained unchanged since, representing roughly 86% of the English Wikipedia's article count.

By early 2026 the site was described as version 0.2, with AI-reviewed user correction suggestions as its headline improvement. The suggestion queue became the system's public pulse: hundreds of thousands of proposed corrections accumulated, visible to anyone watching the review pipeline.

The freeze: April to September 2026

In August 2026, the publication Lawfare reported that Grokipedia had effectively stopped updating. Its investigators examined 34,519 pages and found no changes in more than three months; the last verifiable activity dated to April 24, 2026, and the accumulated correction suggestions sat unprocessed in "in review" queues. Observers on Reddit and X openly questioned whether the project had been abandoned. xAI did not respond to requests for comment.

Updates reportedly resumed in late September 2026: the site's change log showed fresh activity, pages began carrying new "Fact-checked by Grok" stamps, and some user suggestions started clearing again — though reporting described the revival as patchy, with many refreshed pages showing only generic re-verification notices. The cause of the freeze was never publicly explained.

Reception: what reviewers and researchers found

Press and research coverage of Grokipedia's content has been predominantly critical, and readers should know its shape — stated here as reported allegations, not settled fact. Multiple reviewers described the site's framing as promoting right-wing perspectives and Musk's own views. Coverage alleged the validation of positions rejected by scientific consensus on topics including vaccines, climate change, and HIV/AIDS, and accused the site of whitewashing far-right extremism. Studies cited in coverage found reliance on low-credibility sources — including social media clips and fringe forums — particularly on political topics, and noted promotional tone toward far-right figures.

Reaction from Wikipedia's own founders was mixed. Larry Sanger said the jury was still out and allowed it might prove better, noting Grokipedia's article about him contained correct material missing from Wikipedia — while also observing Grok stating things that were not true. Jimmy Wales expressed skepticism about whether large language models are suited to writing encyclopedias at all.

What remains unknown

The honest list of unknowns is long, and worth stating because speculation rushes to fill it. xAI has not published: the methodology behind Grok's article generation or its "fact-checked" reviews; the criteria by which user suggestions are approved or declined; the cause of the 2026 update freeze or the scope of the resumed pipeline; traffic or usage figures beyond third-party estimates; or any roadmap for the project's future. Musk's public silence on the project since February 2026 — as widely reported — leaves even the company's level of commitment a matter of inference.

This opacity is itself the story's most durable fact. An encyclopedia whose operator does not explain how it works must be read with the methods this site teaches: check freshness signals, inspect bibliographies, verify load-bearing claims, and triangulate contested framing against transparent sources.

How to follow this story

Grokipedia is best tracked through three channels: the site's own change logs and article counters, which show what the pipeline is actually doing; independent investigations like Lawfare's, which audit what the site claims; and xAI's rare public statements, which remain the only window into intent. Treat Musk's posts as announcements of direction, press investigations as evidence of condition, and the site itself as a primary source about its own contents — useful, checkable, and never self-validating. The facts above are current as of early October 2026; given how quickly this story has moved — from launch to six million articles to a five-month freeze and back — verify before you rely.

FAQ

Who owns Grokipedia?

Grokipedia was built and is operated by xAI, Elon Musk's AI company, and its articles are generated by xAI's Grok model. xAI has published little formal documentation about the project's internal operations.

Is Grokipedia still being updated?

Public reporting indicates updates effectively stopped from late April to late September 2026 — documented by Lawfare's investigation — and resumed in late September 2026, with fresh "Fact-checked by Grok" stamps appearing on pages. Reporting describes the revival as patchy; xAI has not publicly explained the freeze.

Is this official Grokipedia documentation?

How often should this topic be checked?
